## 1. Before You Tour Acreage in Clark County: Start With the Parcel

Search intent: buying acreage in Clark County, Washington; what to check before touring land.

### Start with what you want to do

A listing can show an attractive piece of land without answering the question that matters most: will it work for your plans? Before scheduling a visit, write down what you hope to use the property for. A home, a workshop, a garden and additional outdoor space create different questions. Keep that list beside the listing so you can separate the features you like from the details you still need to establish.

### Match the listing to the parcel

Ask for the parcel number and confirm that the records you are reviewing correspond to the property being offered. Clark County's Property Information Center and MapsOnline are starting points for parcel research. The county explains that these tools connect with its permitting system and can show permits issued for a property. Save the records and note the date you checked them. A map is a useful research tool; it is not a substitute for resolving a boundary question with the appropriate professional.

### Turn unknowns into specific questions

Instead of asking only whether land is “buildable,” describe your intended project and ask which agency or professional can evaluate it. Make a list covering access, water, wastewater, power and any existing structures. Ask which answers are documented, which need an inspection and which require agency review. Do not treat an attractive aerial image or an acreage figure as proof that your whole plan is feasible.

### Look at systems as well as scenery

If a property has an on-site septic system, request the available records and discuss their relevance with a qualified local professional. Clark County Public Health provides access to septic information and lists certified service providers. An existing system is a topic to investigate, not an automatic answer about what a future project can support. Keep the system questions separate from the listing's general description.

### Bring a useful packet to the conversation

Collect the listing link, parcel number, your intended uses, available documents and the unanswered questions. That small packet makes a conversation with Brad more productive than a folder of favorite photos alone. It also helps identify which questions belong with the broker and which need a surveyor, inspector, utility provider or permitting agency. The aim is to understand the property before allowing an assumption to become part of your plan.

## 2. How to Compare Clark County Communities Before Choosing a Home

Search intent: comparing Vancouver, Ridgefield, Camas, Battle Ground, Brush Prairie and Washougal.

### Start with a normal Tuesday

A community can make a great impression during a weekend visit. Choosing a home calls for a second kind of visit: one that looks like an ordinary day. Write down the destinations you use regularly and the times you expect to travel. Then test those routes from actual candidate addresses. A broad city label cannot tell you how your own routine will work.

### Choose places you would use

Use local amenities to make your visits specific. Vancouver Waterfront Park connects with the Columbia River Renaissance Trail. Ridgefield's city trail guide includes Abrams Park and refuge connections. Camas has the Round Lake and Lacamas park network. These are useful starting points for a visit, but the right comparison is the trip from the particular home to the public entrance you would use. Nearby on a map does not always mean convenient on foot.

### Give each property the same questions

Record your priorities before touring: the space you need, outdoor upkeep you are prepared for, storage, parking and the features you cannot compromise on. Ask the same questions at each property. Separate what you observed from what someone needs to confirm. A useful note might say “check the shared driveway documents,” rather than assuming how maintenance works because the driveway looks well cared for.

### Verify the details that follow the address

Confirm school assignment with the relevant district and service availability with the providers using the exact address. For property records, start with the appropriate local agency. Do not assume that a community name or mailing address answers every jurisdiction or service question. Keep links to the sources you consulted so you can return to them when a specific property becomes a serious candidate.

### Keep your comparison small enough to use

Choose two or three communities for the next round rather than trying to rank the entire county at once. Use the website's community comparison tool and buyer checklist to capture what you learned. Bring Brad your candidate listings, the routes you tested and the remaining questions. That gives the conversation a practical focus: which properties deserve another look, and what information do you need next?
