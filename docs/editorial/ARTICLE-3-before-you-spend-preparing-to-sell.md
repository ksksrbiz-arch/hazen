## Article 3: Getting Ready to Sell in Clark County? Make a Plan Before You Start Spending

**Proposed URL:** `/blog/before-you-spend-preparing-to-sell/`

**Search description:** Before ordering paint, replacing finishes or scheduling major work, organize your goals, property records and questions. A focused preparation plan starts there.

### Start with the move you are trying to make

A seller’s to-do list can grow quickly: paint, flooring, landscaping, repairs, photographs. Before booking the work, step back and write down your timing, the amount of work you can manage and the questions you want answered about the sale.

Bring those details to an early conversation with Brad. A property that needs to be ready soon presents different decisions from one with time for a longer preparation schedule. The first useful question is what needs attention for this property and this move.

### Sort the list before you price the list

Use three headings: condition questions, presentation and optional changes. Put something you do not understand—such as a recurring leak—under condition questions. Put clearing a cluttered room under presentation. Put replacing a functioning finish simply because you prefer another style under optional changes.

This is a conversation tool, not a recommendation to leave a problem untreated. It helps you explain what you know and where you need advice. Ask what each proposed project would accomplish, who should evaluate it and how it fits your timing and budget.

### Collect records while you plan the work

Make a folder for records you already have: permits, service reports, warranties and information about completed improvements. Note anything you cannot find. Clark County offers online property and permit research, and explains that records not available online may need a separate request.

If the home uses a septic system, raise that early. Clark County Public Health advises sellers and real estate professionals to have a current Report of System Status on file before listing. Check the county’s current guidance and discuss the property with an appropriate certified provider instead of leaving the question until the end of preparation.

### Make the property easier to understand

A useful presentation lets a buyer see how the rooms and outdoor areas work. Walk through the home with that purpose in mind. Where does stored material hide space? What would you need to explain during a visit? Which features deserve a clear photograph?

Use the listing-photo guide to plan the views you want to discuss. Photographs should help explain the property accurately. Keep questions about condition and documents on their own list; a good-looking image does not answer them.

### Agree on the next few actions

You do not need a long list of projects to have a productive first meeting. Bring a short description of your timing, the work you are considering, available records and the questions you cannot answer. Ask Brad to help you organize the next conversation and identify where specialist advice is needed.

For each action you agree on, record who is handling it and what has to happen before the next step. Use the seller checklist to keep the plan together. The goal is a preparation process you understand, with decisions made deliberately rather than a rush of spending before the listing conversation.
